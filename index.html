The story behind the kete

HWoven by Hine, a kairaranga (weaver) from Te Tai Tokerau. Shared with her permission.

Raranga (weaving) is mātauranga Māori — knowledge passed down from one generation to the next. Hine learned it from her kuia (grandmother).

Tikanga for harvesting harakeke

Only the outer leaves are cut. The centre shoot (te rito) and the two leaves beside it (ngā awhi rito) are never cut — like a child protected by its parents — so the plant keeps growing. Leftover harakeke goes back to the base of the plant.

Part of every sale goes back to Hine and her whānau.

Words: kete — woven bag · harakeke — NZ flax · kuia — grandmother, female elder
